Treble clef notes chart

The treble clef is also called the G clef. Its inner curl wraps around the second line from the bottom and fixes that line as G4, the G just above middle C. Every other note follows from there in alphabetical order, alternating line, space, line: the space above G is A, the middle line is B, the next space is C, and so on. After G the letters start again at A.

Lines and spaces: the mnemonics

LinesE G B D FE4 G4 B4 D5 F5Every Good Boy Does Fine
SpacesF A C EF4 A4 C5 E5Spells FACE

Two more notes sit just outside the five lines without needing a ledger line: D4 hangs in the space below the bottom line and G5 sits in the space above the top line. Those 11 notes, D4 to G5, are level 3 of the game.

Landmark notes: faster than mnemonics

Mnemonics get you started, but reciting "Every Good Boy…" for every note is slow in real music. Fluent readers know a few landmark notes on sight and read everything else as a step (line to the next space) or a skip (line to the next line) away from one:

  • Treble G (G4): the second line, where the clef curls.
  • Middle C (C4): one ledger line below the staff.
  • Treble C (C5): the third space, an octave above middle C.
  • The outer lines: E4 on the bottom line and F5 on the top line.
  • High C (C6): two ledger lines above the staff.

From G, one step up is A and one skip up is B. From C5, one step down is B4. The same thinking in distances is what intervals are made of, which is why reading practice and interval ear training help each other.

Ledger lines above and below the treble staff

Ledger lines are short extra lines that continue the staff's line-space-line pattern. Below the treble staff: D4 in the space, C4 (middle C) on the first ledger line, B3 just under it and A3 on the second ledger line. Above: G5 in the space, A5 on the first ledger line, B5 just above it and C6 on the second ledger line. Count out from the nearest staff line or landmark rather than counting ledger lines from scratch. Very high passages are often written an octave lower with an 8va sign to save ledger lines.

A sharp (♯) raises a note by a semitone and a flat (♭) lowers it by one. The letter name still comes from the line or space, so a sharp on the G line is G♯. Level 5 of the game adds them. Key signatures are the next step; the interactive circle of fifths shows which sharps or flats belong to each key.

Who reads treble clef?

Treble clef covers the upper range: violin, flute, oboe, clarinet, saxophones, trumpet, most horn parts, mandolin, soprano and alto voices, and the right hand of the piano. Guitar and tenor voice parts are also written in treble clef but sound an octave lower than written (often shown with a small 8 under the clef). If you also play piano or a low instrument, move on to bass clef notes and the grand staff.

How to practise treble clef notes with the game

  • Practice mode is untimed and endless. Tap the letter (or press A–G on a keyboard), hear the note, and get the right answer plus a mnemonic or landmark hint when you miss. Tap Hint before answering if you are stuck.
  • Levels build up: line notes, then space notes, the whole staff, ledger lines with middle C, and finally sharps and flats. Get 13 of your last 15 notes right and the next level unlocks, but you can pick any level at any time.
  • Sprint mode gives you 60 seconds. Every 5 correct answers in a row raises your combo multiplier, up to x4, and a miss resets it. Your best score for each level is saved, and you can copy your result to share it.
  • Notes you miss come back more often. The game weights its picks towards the notes you get wrong, and the note map under the game colours every line and space by your accuracy, so you can see your weakest note at a glance.

A few minutes a day works better than one long session (see spaced repetition for musicians). Go for accuracy first; speed follows. Say the note name out loud before you tap, and when a level feels easy, mix it up: interleaved practice is harder in the moment but sticks better.

Frequently asked questions

What are the notes of the treble clef?

The five lines from bottom to top are E, G, B, D and F (E4 to F5). The four spaces are F, A, C and E (F4 to E5). Just outside the staff are D4 below and G5 above, and with ledger lines the treble clef continues down through middle C and up to C6 and beyond.

How do you remember the treble clef notes?

Lines: Every Good Boy Does Fine (E G B D F). Spaces: they spell FACE. After that, learn a few landmark notes, such as G on the second line and middle C on the first ledger line below, and read other notes as steps from them.

Where is middle C on the treble clef?

Middle C (C4) sits on the first ledger line below the treble staff. The D just above it hangs in the space under the bottom line.

Why is the treble clef called the G clef?

The treble clef grew out of a stylised letter G, and its curl wraps around the second line to mark that line as G4, the G above middle C.

What is the difference between treble clef and bass clef notes?

The same line or space is a different note in each clef. The bass clef lines are G B D F A and the spaces are A C E G, so each bass clef position is named two letters higher than the treble clef position (E becomes G, F becomes A) and sounds much lower.
