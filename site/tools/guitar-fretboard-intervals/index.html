Compare the two right-hand columns: from the D string, any shape that reaches the B string sits one fret higher. From the G string, every shape that crosses to the B string shifts too, so a major 3rd from the G string is on the B string at the same fret.

Which intervals to learn first

  1. Perfect 5th and octave. Root, 5th and octave are the power chord, and the octave shape lets you find any note again two strings higher.
  2. Major and minor 3rds. The 3rd decides whether a chord is major or minor. Spot it inside the chord shapes you already know.
  3. The rest of the major scale. 2nd, 4th, 6th and 7th. With these you can build any scale or arpeggio from a root.
  4. Minor intervals and the tritone. The minor 7th makes dominant 7th and minor 7th chords; the tritone is the tension inside every dominant chord.

How to practise

  1. Say it as you play it. Play a root, then the interval, and name it out loud: "root, major 3rd".
  2. Find chord tones. Pick a root on the low E string and find its 3rd, 5th and 7th nearby. That's an arpeggio, and it's where interval shapes pay off.
  3. Move roots around. The shapes stay the same from any fret, so practise from roots all over the neck, then on the D and G strings to learn the B-string shift.
  4. Listen, don't just look. Knowing the shape of a major 6th doesn't mean you can hear one. The interval ear trainer trains that by ear, and the shapes and sounds reinforce each other.

Frequently asked questions

What is an interval on guitar?

An interval is the distance between two notes, counted in half steps. On guitar one fret is one half step, so every interval has a fixed shape on the neck: a major 3rd is always four half steps above the root, whether that's four frets up the same string or one string up and one fret back.

What are the most important guitar intervals to learn first?

The perfect 5th and octave (the power chord), then the major and minor 3rds, which decide whether a chord is major or minor. After that, learn the rest of the major scale (2nd, 4th, 6th, 7th), then the minor intervals and the tritone.

Why do interval shapes change on the B string?

In standard tuning each string is a perfect 4th (5 half steps) above the one below it, except the B string, which is a major 3rd (4 half steps) above the G string. Any shape that crosses from the G string to the B string moves up one fret to make up the difference.

How do I find a major 3rd on guitar?

From a root on the low E, A or D string, go one string up and one fret back, or four frets up the same string. From a root on the G string, the major 3rd is on the B string at the same fret, because of the shorter G–B gap.

Where is the octave on the guitar?

From a root on the low E or A string, go two strings up and two frets up. From the D or G string, go two strings up and three frets up, because the shape crosses the G–B pair.
