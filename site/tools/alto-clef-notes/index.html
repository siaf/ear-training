Alto clef notes chart

The alto clef is a C clef. The two curves of the clef meet in a point, and whatever line that point sits on is middle C. In the alto clef it points at the middle (third) line, so the staff is centred on middle C, reaching from F3 on the bottom line to G4 on the top line. That's why it suits instruments whose range sits around middle C.

Alto clef has no single standard mnemonic the way treble does, so borrow what you know: the lines start with FACE (the treble clef spaces) and the spaces are the first four bass clef lines. Just outside the staff are E3 below and A4 above.

If you already read treble clef, there's a shortcut you can use as a check: each alto clef position is one letter higher than the same position in treble clef, an octave lower. The treble bottom line is E4; the alto bottom line is F3. Use it to confirm, not as your main method: the goal is to see the note directly.

Landmark notes and the viola strings

  • Middle C (C4): the middle line, where the clef points. Your anchor for everything else.
  • The outer lines: F3 on the bottom line and G4 on the top line.
  • The viola's open strings: C3 just below the first ledger line under the staff, G3 in the bottom space, D4 in the third space and A4 in the space above the staff.
  • C5: just above the first ledger line above the staff, an octave above the middle line.

Level 1 of the game drills the five notes around middle C (A3 to E4), so you build the habit of reading out from the clef's centre. After that come lines, spaces, the whole staff, ledger lines (down to B2, past the open C string, and up to D5) and accidentals.

Which instruments use the alto clef?

The viola is the main one: viola music is written in alto clef, switching to treble clef for long high passages. The alto clef is also used for alto trombone, and you'll meet it in older vocal and choral editions, where alto parts were traditionally written with a C clef. Because the clef is centred on middle C, a viola's range fits on the staff with few ledger lines; in treble clef the lower notes would need several below, and in bass clef the upper notes several above.

Cellists, bassoonists and trombonists read a different C clef, the tenor clef, where middle C is the fourth line. Practising both helps keep them apart: the hub's mixed-clef game shuffles treble, bass, alto and tenor.

Frequently asked questions

What are the notes of the alto clef?

The five lines from bottom to top are F, A, C, E and G (F3 to G4), with middle C on the middle line. The four spaces are G, B, D and F (G3 to F4). E3 sits just below the staff and A4 just above it.

Where is middle C on the alto clef?

On the middle (third) line. The alto clef is a C clef, and the point where its two curves meet marks middle C.

Which instruments use the alto clef?

Mainly the viola, which reads alto clef and switches to treble clef for high passages. It is also used for alto trombone and appears in older vocal and choral editions for alto parts.

How do I read alto clef if I already read treble clef?

Each alto clef position is one letter higher than the same position in treble clef, sounding an octave lower: the bottom line is F3 instead of E4. Use that as a check, but aim to read alto clef directly from its landmarks, starting with middle C on the middle line.

Why does the viola use the alto clef?

The viola's range sits around middle C. Centring the staff on middle C keeps most viola notes on the staff; in treble clef the low notes would need several ledger lines, and in bass clef the high ones would.
